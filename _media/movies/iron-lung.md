MAJOR inspiration for me right here in multiple respects. 

iron lung itself (the game) was a really big source of inspiration for my gamedev journey a few years back. the fact that such a simple game, with a simple premise, was so successful because of good _worldbuilding_ and _atmosphere_ made david szymanski basically a role model for me. 

and the fact that it was turned into a movie, by essentially an indie filmmaker (ignoring the whole youtuber part), and i was WATCHING IT IN SWEDEN (it was supposed to be US only), blew my mind. it single-handedly triggered my continuation of development on [tracerouter](/projects/tracerouter). i remember the very next day, deciding that i would make a new release, and opening up the project for the first time in months. 

the movie itself was awesome. i totally get why some people feel like it dragged on, for the same reason people felt like backrooms dragged on. the filmmakers believe in the atmosphere and commit to it; it's what the original game did. it's like an electronic trance song: the repetition gives you the space to sink _into_ the layers and become something else entirely. yes, it wouldn't be the easiest rewatch. but going in for the first time, it felt like a great pace. 

i also get why people felt like there wasn't enough explanation of the lore. i even felt a pang of that. but i understand why it wouldn't fit into the movie. and i also get why people didn't like it because of the acting. mark isn't a professional actor and he happens to be basically the only one on screen for 2 hours.

i think the review by hbomberguy on letterboxd that Mark read out sums it up better than i ever could.
