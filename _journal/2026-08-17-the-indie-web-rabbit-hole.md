It's day 2 of my website redesign and I'm already overwhelmed by the lingo.

That's not to say it hasn't been going well. I've been switching between poking around _Neocities_ sites and piecing together my own, building up a design language and workflow for CSS art. 

<div class="blog_image right">
    <img src="/img/journal/blinkies.png">
    <p>My own little collection of <i>blinkies</i>.</p>
</div>

So, to sum it up: there's this whole movement that's been going on for the past decade or so called the _Indie Web_---a pushback against the _corporate web_ through the forms of personal, off-the-grid, anti-algorithm websites symbolized by that zany retro 90s aesthetic of the early internet. It's simultaneously self-aware of the current state of social media and, for lack of a better word, role-playing as living in that era. It treats websites as digital bedrooms, usually being decorated with the web-equivalent of posters, bookshelves, and sticky notes that say what the owner is up to. Contrary to what I had been mostly designing before, these websurfers aren't building a portfolio. They're not trying to sell anything to anyone. They're _collecting_ experiences from the internet---books, websites, gifs, movies, trends, art---and decorating a corner to be themselves in.

These netizens share around "blinkies"---little 88x31 gifs---like eccentric jokes; there are some archives of them, like on <a href="https://thuvien.org/webcore/blinkies">thuvien.org</a>, but they're usually found by browsing other indie websites and accumulating them over time. Of course, they're not to be confused with "buttons," "badges," or "stamps," which I'm sure have some nuanced difference between each other. There are also "webshrines," which are highly curated pages devoted to specific pieces of art or media (<a href="https://altalenae.neocities.org/shrines/arrival">altalenae has a good one for Arrival</a>), and which I can very much see doing for Ian Hubert or Hawaii Pt. II down the road.

Some other websites I've liked (which I do want to move to a _node_ later on) are:
- [irony-machine](https://irony-machine.neocities.org/)
- [lazybones](https://lazybones.neocities.org/)

As someone who treats Minecraft servers as third places, who has a soft spot for interactive text adventures, and who has had a building resentment of modern social media, I can absolutely feel myself falling in love with this treatment of the internet. I truly believe that everyone---at least, everyone who currently occupies themselves with social media---should have a personal site. It doesn't have to display one's deepest fears or love lives. But it importantly shifts the frame of viewing others lives away from glancing through windows past poster ads from a train seat and instead towards stepping inside someone's home for a little bit and catching up over tea. On these sites, you're not battling against an algorithm or the weighty momentum of your audience's scrolling. And that's important. To me, at least.
