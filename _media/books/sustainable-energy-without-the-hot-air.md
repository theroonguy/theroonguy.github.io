really great foundational book on sustainable energy systems, centered around the question of if we can conceivably live on 100% sustainable energy without sacrificing quality of life. mackay has a very straight-to-the-point yet easy to read way of speaking which has influenced the way i write about similar topics. he boiled everything down---from nuclear power plants to the energy required to bike every day---to the same units: kWh per day per person, which made it really interesting to compare, say, how much energy it takes to produce a burger versus flying an international flight. the book summarizes all different sorts of ways of capturing, storing, and using energy, which really puts into perspective how we'll need a lot of everything to "solve" climate change. as he says:

> "If everyone does a little, we'll only achieve a little."

as expected, it's sobering. especially being an american. we emit 24 tons of CO2 a year per person, compared to the 2-3 tons from the "polluted countries" like china and india (not to mention how much manufacturing we outsource to them). 

mackay does provide some particularly interesting solutions for the future. he makes a very compelling case that electric cars are a necessity, but also posits the theory that the millions of chargers charging them could adjust their power usage dynamically to make up for the intermittency of renewables. or his idea for congestion-charging, where every car tracks proximity to other vehicles (rather than location, which is controversial), and gets taxed based on that amount. 

overall, very solid book that more than anything clarifies and fills in the gaps of the basics of sustainable energy and what the future might look like if we as a species have enough collective self-preservation instinct.
