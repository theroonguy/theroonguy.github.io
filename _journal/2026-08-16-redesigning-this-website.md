My website is in a bit of an identity crisis. 

It doesn't know whether it's a personal website or a professional one. It doesn't know if it should be retro-stylized corner-of-the-internet or a sleek and minimalistic portfolio archive. If it should house refined essays and reflections or stream-of-consciousness ideas. And it certainly doesn't know if it should be viewed on iPhones. 

Today I want to redesign it to *actually* fit my needs. I won't be able to finish everything---if anything, this website will constantly be evolved over time---but I at least want a structure that matches what comes out of my brain and what should go into others.

My website is rather unique in that I need to present the same parts of myself in different ways. For example, I built a <a href="/projects/cat_tower">6-foot tall, 140 lb cat tower</a> earlier this summer. Ultimately, it's a personal passion project that I built for myself and my family and my first real venture into woodworking. But it also happens to be a portfolio piece for my mechanical engineering career---a concrete example of something I designed, priced, and constructed through sheer self-motivation. It's one project, but there are two audiences: a friend or family spectator who might be interested in my life, or a potential employer who only has the time to skim what matters. And the gap between the two only widens when I get to discussing my development for <a href="/projects/tracerouter">my game Tracerouter</a> or Blender projects. What things do I want my employers to see, or to not see? My friends and family? Members of the internet?

To start, I need a clearer separation of the content I share here. I'm planning on dividing it into 4 categories, which can be thought of as a range from evolving, time-independent pages to momentary blips of ideas:

1. **Nodes**: Persistent, ever-evolving pages or hubs about topics or concepts from my life. Think: photo collections, my views on the energy industry, or my list of all-time favorite video games.
2. **Projects**: Substantial things I've created or worked on, professional or not. The project may conclude, but the notes may continue to be refined.
3. **Writings**: Essays, guides, or any other well-thought-out arrangements of words I'm confident enough to attach my name to.
4. **Journal**: About as informal as it gets here on this site! From a couple sentences to a word wall, this are thoughts that are one layer about my actual handheld journal. This page counts as one.

Now, instead of having a uniform aesthetic that is mediocre at both expressing my personality for the more personal pages and being straight-to-the-point for potential employers, I'm planning on creating a wider range of styles that varies based on formality. The home page needs to have clear directions and an immediate understanding of my personality and capabilities. I've gotten inspired by websites on Neocities, such as <a href="https://altalenae.neocities.org/">altalenae's</a>, for how well they capture the feeling of the "indie web" and the eccentric and passionate persona they express.

What I particularly like:
- "Bookshelf" section, with favorite books, articles, and reviews. 
- "Currently" sections, such as what they're currently reading, or doing.
- Plenty of links on the home page with icons.

While I do really appreciate the retro aesthetic of all these Neocities websites, I'd prefer for my website to stand out in its own unique way. I've recently been looking into *Russian Constructivism*, in particular the architectural works of *Yakov Chernikhov*. I think it would be interesting to integrate the bold and geometrical style that still references an older time.
